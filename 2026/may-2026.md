# 31 May

I just had the craziest café experience.

Ken and I went on a Bible date in a café nearby. I went to the corner to set up my study Bible while Ken ordered for the both of us. We sat down, settled, and started reading while waiting for our food.

Minutes later, three men sat next to us. We didn’t think anything of it; we just minded our business. While my eyes were glued to my Bible, I strongly felt as if someone was staring at us. I looked around and, true enough, one of the guys was looking at us, seemingly triggered and angered by what we were doing. He murmured something to his mates, then the other two both gave us an angry side-eye and moved to the table at my 1 o’clock, a metre and a half away from me.

That didn’t end there. I was uneasy with the looks they gave us, but I didn’t mention anything to Ken, afraid that we’d start a confrontation of some sort. I just didn’t mind them and continued what I was doing.

Our orders arrived one by one, then we ate, we read, we discussed stuff. I can’t shake off the heaviness I’m continually feeling on me, and that’s when I saw the guy earlier, still staring at me vehemently as if I killed his family and he must avenge them. I locked eyes with him for a brief moment until the server brought their food.

I was briefly transported to a horrifying scene: the guy, holding me at arm’s length, repeatedly punches me until I am out of breath. I, on the other hand, had this unnatural strength and grit, that although I knew that I could fight him back, I didn't. Albeit the blood marred on my face, I didn't feel tired or defeated. It's as if I had the upper hand on the fight; it just didn't feel like it.

I haven’t seen anyone stare me to death at all. Mind you, I haven’t met this guy before; I haven’t done anything towards him on that café encounter to credit his behaviour. I was just there peacefully reading my Bible, highlighting and taking notes with my love.

Minutes dragged on, and the three of them left. I breathed out a sigh of relief as the atmosphere lightened. I was about to tell Ken what happened when he mentioned that those three guys looked agitated at us. He mentioned he had a gut feeling they were from a certain religion, but he didn’t want to assume and wanted to make sure. His assumptions were proven true when the three of them left the table beside us and went somewhere. Ken wasn’t aware that they sat on a table behind him; he just felt their anger seething through.

For context: We’re living in a suburb with people from the East, South, South-East Asia, Middle East, Europe, and a few Aussies here and there. 

The men were of Middle Eastern descent.

I then thought of our Christian brothers and sisters in Nigeria who deal with this about their daily lives to the extreme degree. I hadn’t anticipated experiencing such violence in my own neighbourhood.  Living in a country where we can freely worship God is truly a privilege.

I was deeply shaken for an hour after that encounter.
