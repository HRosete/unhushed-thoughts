Just when I thought that my career was over, God pulled the trigger.

I left my previous job burnt out, wrung dry, and too salty even to say goodbye. I even vowed to never work in engineering again, fearing that such instances that led to my situation might happen once more.

# 17 September

Day 4 of working back in tech, and I almost went home at the 6.5-hour mark.

Scrap that - I went home 30 minutes before my usual log-off time.

I was stressed and overwhelmed with everything going on: a lot of new libraries to learn (which I love doing; I learned a lot and even giggled when I finally got one working), a codebase that's all over the place and hard to understand, impostor syndrome, feeling like a fraud (now doubting my past semiconductor experience), and total information overload.

I started asking the Lord Jesus why He answered my prayers! I felt like a fool for asking Him that, but I truly felt like I had bitten off more than I could chew. Then I remembered that I didn't actively clamor for this job, nor did I idolize it from the job ad, hoping to get hired. It was actually my dear husband who applied for this job on my behalf. He later admitted that he had even forgotten he applied for me! Neither of us had a clue until I asked the software lead, Owen, where he found my profile—to which he answered, "Ah, good question. We found you on, uh, Seek!"

Incoming word vomit (the things that made me want to pack my bags and leave mid-shift):

> Stressors: An untidy codebase, new libraries to study, feeling insecure because I can't grasp them instantly, everything feeling harder than it should be, feeling like a fraud, doubting my past experience, and impostor syndrome (which made me want to walk out 6.5 hours in).

> Dynamic: Working on my own, but my TL opened his Slack channel if I had questions. I wanted to try on my own first before asking (I do ask every day; today I just got overwhelmed big time).

> Context: My 3.5-year gap consisted of 2 years doing a master's degree while working non-technical jobs.


I walked distraught from the office to the station and called my husband to tell him I took off 30 minutes early. He responded right away and took me to Burwood for snacks before heading home.

It took me a while to process my emotions. I felt numb, but mentally I knew something was terribly off. I had suppressed my feelings until I tried to make light of the situation by joking that maybe I was a diversity or personality hire. That thought stings because I once heard my first boss explicitly say that to my friend's team lead, who had included me in his list because of the skills he saw in my undergraduate degree. I joked that they chose the wrong person for the job, or that I shouldn't go back tomorrow and should just resign. I felt horrible. 

As the bus took its route home, tears fell and I sobbed the whole way back. Those sobs turned into heavy heaves when my husband wrapped his arms around me tenderly, empathizing with everything I felt.
